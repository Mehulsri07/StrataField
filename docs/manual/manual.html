StrataField
User Manual

How to keep borewell records, bring in your Excel drilling logs, see the water level across Lucknow, and keep your data safe.

The pictures in this manual use made-up borewells. Your screens will show your own.

Three things worth knowing first

  • Everything is saved on this computer as you go. There is no Save button to forget, except where a screen has its own (for example Save borewell).
  • All depths are in feet. Logs written in metres are converted when you import them.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. Only the street map and address look-up use the internet.

Needs attention

On Home, Needs attention lists things worth fixing: borewells with no location, with no soil layers, with an owner, water level or date missing, soil names that were not recognised, and backups that are getting old. Click a line to go straight to the borewells it means.

2. Add a borewell by hand

Choose New borewell. There are six short steps; only the ID is required.

  1. Basics. The borewell's ID, the owner's name, the zone and the date it was drilled. For the zone, pick one you have used before or type a new name.
  2. Location. Type the area, then give the position in whichever way is easiest:
    • paste or type coordinates copied from a phone or Google Maps, for example 26.8742, 80.9480;
    • click Pick on the map and click the spot;
    • click Use a photo's GPS and choose a photo taken at the site;
    • click Look up the address for an approximate position (needs internet).
  3. Drilling & water. Total depth, water level, hole size and pipe size.
  4. Layers & pipes. Click Add layer for each soil layer and type where it starts and ends. The drawing on the right fills in as you go.
  5. Photos & files. Optional. Site photos, the drilling log, permits.
  6. Check & save. Read it through, then click Save borewell.

Gaps between layers. If a depth was not logged, do not guess. Click Mark 40–50 ft as not recorded. It is drawn as "Not recorded" and is never filled in by estimates.

You can stop part-way. If you close StrataField before saving, the next time you open New borewell it picks up where you left off.

3. Bring in Excel drilling logs

Choose Import Excel, then Choose Excel files. You can choose many files at once. Nothing is saved until you press Import.

StrataField reads the soil layers, the plain pipe and screens, the site name, address, date, hole and pipe sizes and the water level. Rows of the same soil are joined into one layer. Logs in metres are converted to feet.

The list on the left shows each file as:

It saysWhat it means
ReadyEverything was found. Have a quick look and import.
Needs a lookIt can be imported, but something is noted: no water level in the file, depths converted from metres, a second log on the same sheet. Read the notes; type in anything missing if you know it.
Check the columnsThe file is laid out differently, so StrataField guessed where things are. See below.
Cannot be readThe file could not be opened. Add the borewell by hand and attach the file to it.

Untick a file to leave it out. Then click Import … borewells. The original Excel file is kept with each borewell, under its Files tab.

A file laid out differently

Under Where the layers are in this file, check that each box points at the right column: where a layer ends, the soil name, the first row with a layer, and, if the file has them, where a layer starts and the pipe type. Say whether the depths are in feet or metres.

After importing

An imported borewell has no position on the map, and may have no owner, water level or date if the file did not say. To find them, open Borewells and click No location or Missing details. The notes from the Import screen are kept in each borewell's History tab.

4. Find a borewell

Open Borewells, or press Ctrl + K from anywhere and start typing.

  • Search looks in the ID, owner, area, address and zone.
  • All zones and Any soil type narrow the list to one zone, or to borewells that have a given soil.
  • More filters adds depth, water level and date ranges.
  • The coloured strip in each row is the borewell's layers from top to bottom; the blue line is the water level. Click a colour to see that layer.
  • Status says what a record still lacks, for example "No location" or "No owner, water level".

5. A borewell's page

The tabs across the top are:

  • Overview: the details, owner and location, the layers, and the drawing.
  • Layers & pipes: the layers and pipe pieces as tables.
  • Water: every water reading. Add a new one whenever the level is measured again; the borewell always shows the newest.
  • Photos and Files: add site photos, the drilling log or permits. They are copied into StrataField's own folder, so the originals can be moved or deleted.
  • History: every change to this borewell, and the notes from when it was imported.

The buttons at the top right are Edit details, Edit layers & pipes, Make PDF report, and the bin, which moves the borewell to the Recycle bin.

6. Change layers and pipes

On a borewell's page, click Edit layers & pipes.

  • Type where each layer starts and ends, in feet.
  • To change a soil: click the row, then click a soil on the left. Or use the box in the row.
  • Tick Holds water for a layer that gave water.
  • Under Pipes, add each piece and choose Plain pipe or Screen pipe (where water enters).
  • Changes are saved as you type; the top right says All changes saved. Undo and Redo step back and forward.

If two layers cover the same depth, or a layer ends above where it starts, the row is marked and the change is not saved until it is fixed.

7. The map

  • Click a dot to see that borewell; click its name in the list on the right to find it on the map.
  • A circle with a number is several borewells close together. Click it to zoom in.
  • The colours between borewells are estimates, and fade out far from any borewell.
  • Water depth colours turns the colours off and on. Show all zooms back out to every borewell.

Which water readings count

Water levels measured years apart cannot be compared, because the water moves. The box at the top (Readings from the last 3 years) chooses which readings the colours use: the last year, 3 years, 5 years, or all of them. Borewells with older readings stay on the map as grey dots. The same choice is used on Home.

Several borewells at the same spot

Without internet. The dots and water colours always work. To see streets as well, download the Lucknow map once: Settings & backup → Map without internet.

8. Cross-sections

A cross-section shows the soil layers underground along a line, as if the ground were cut open.

  1. Choose Cross-section. Click the map where the line should start, then where it should end. Or try North to south or West to east.
  2. Drag A or A′ to move the ends. Drag the small dot in the middle of the line to bend it, for example to follow a road. Double-click a bend to remove it.
  3. The slider sets how far from the line a borewell may be and still be used.

Only the columns are measured. Between two borewells StrataField joins matching layers with straight lines. The real layer may be deeper, shallower or broken. The further apart the borewells, the rougher the estimate; beyond about 5 km nothing is drawn.

Save keeps the line for next time. Save as PDF and Save as picture make a file to print or send. Under More options you can show heights above sea level, so the borewells stand at their real ground height.

9. Make PDF reports and Excel files

  • PDF report: one page per borewell, with its drawing, layers, pipes and water readings. Good for printing and for giving to the owner.
  • Excel workbook: one file with sheets for borewells, soil layers, pipes and water readings.

Choose All borewells, One zone, or Choose borewells and tick the ones you want. Click the Save button and choose where to put the file. For a single borewell, Make PDF report on its own page is quicker.

10. Backups

StrataField makes a backup by itself every day and keeps the last 10. You do not have to do anything.

  • Back up now makes one straight away, for example before a big import.
  • Restore puts everything back to how it was when that backup was made. A copy of how things are now is saved first, so a restore can itself be undone.

Set up a second copy. Backups on this computer are lost if the computer breaks or is stolen. Under A second copy of your backups, choose a folder on a USB drive, or one that OneDrive or Google Drive keeps in the cloud. Every backup is then copied there too. Home tells you if the copies stop, for example when the USB drive is not plugged in.

To move to a new computer: install StrataField there, copy a backup file across, and use Restore from a file….

11. Zones and soil types

  • Rename a zone to fix its spelling: click Rename, type, Save.
  • Merge two zones that are the same place: rename one to the other's name. StrataField asks first, then moves all its borewells across.
  • Soil names not recognised lists names from Excel files that match no soil type. Choose what each one means and every layer with that name is fixed together.
  • Soil types is the list you choose from for a layer. Add your own with Add soil type; change the colour or pattern of any with the pencil.

12. See what changed, and undo a delete

Activity is the place to look after a batch of imports: each file's notes are there, and Open borewell takes you to it. Each borewell's own History tab shows the same for just that borewell.

The Recycle bin

Deleting a borewell moves it to the Recycle bin. It no longer shows in lists or on the map, but nothing is lost. Restore puts it back. Delete for good removes it and its photos and files; that cannot be undone except by restoring a backup.

13. Updates

When a newer version is ready, StrataField says so at the top of the screen. Click Install and restart. It takes about a minute: StrataField closes, updates and opens again. Your data is not touched. You can also check at any time under Settings & backup → About → Check for updates.

14. If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
A red message at the bottom rightRead it: it says what went wrong and usually how to fix it. Nothing was saved from that one action; everything before it is safe.
An Excel file shows "Cannot be read"Open it in Excel and save it again as .xlsx, then try once more. Or add the borewell by hand and attach the file.
The layers from an Excel file look wrongOn the Import screen, check the choices under Where the layers are in this file. After importing, use Edit layers & pipes.
A borewell is not on the mapIt has no location. Open it, click Edit details, and add one under Location.
The map is grey, with no streetsThere is no internet. Dots and water colours still work. Download the Lucknow map under Settings to have streets without internet.
Something was deleted by mistakeLook in the Recycle bin. For anything else, restore a backup from before it happened.
"StrataField could not open your data"Do not delete anything. Send the details below to whoever helps you with StrataField.

Asking for help

Go to Settings & backup → About.

  • Copy details for support shows the versions, how much data there is, the state of backups and recent errors. Click Copy and paste it into an email or message. It contains no borewell details, and nothing is sent unless you send it.
  • Open the error log opens the full list of problems recorded on this computer.
  • Open data folder shows where your data is kept, in case you are asked for it.

Your data is in the folder Strata inside your Windows user's AppData folder. Uninstalling StrataField removes the program but leaves that folder, so installing again brings everything back.
